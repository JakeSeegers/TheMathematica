# Research-Statistics RPG — design proposal

Status: **all 5 steps built** (§13); the first investigation plays end to end. Written 2026-10-21 as the "next deliverable" of the project
direction *A Research-Statistics RPG*; the recommendations of §5 were accepted the same day. The
research core (`src/game/research/`) is tested in Node, and the game records fights into it when
a page is opened with `?research=cinderwatch-pass`; the field ledger (L) carries the five
missions, the data and charts, the report and the debrief.
Values marked *placeholder* are open and are only there to show that the design works with real
numbers.

Guiding principle: **combat supplies the evidence; research turns evidence into claims; the
kingdom turns claims into decisions; the consequences create the next research question.**

## 1. The current architecture

- **Engine** (`src/engine/`): three.js 2.5D renderer, input, audio, pixel UI kit (dialog box with
  choices, HUD, journal, toasts), level format, i18n (en / ja / zh), a seeded `RNG`
  (`src/engine/utils/math.js`, string or number seeds). See [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md).
- **Game** (`src/game/`): the play mode — player, villagers, story runner (talk scripts, quests,
  flags, the journal), party of companions, campaigns of several levels with saves
  (`CampaignState`), and — on levels with enemies only — combat. See
  [architecture/GAME.md](../architecture/GAME.md).
- **Combat** (`src/game/glaive/`, since 2026-10-21): turn-based on the GLAIVE rules
  ([specs/GLAIVE.md](../specs/GLAIVE.md)). Pure, Node-safe rules (`dice.js`, `rules.js`,
  `Sheet.js`, `bestiary.js`, `Encounter.js`) produce **beats** (one thing that happened: an
  attack with its roll record, damage, a save, a death). The game side (`GlaiveCombat.js`,
  `Stage.js`, `TurnHUD.js`) animates beats and writes the log. Fights are seeded and
  reproducible; `tools/test-glaive.mjs` tests the rules in Node.
- **The combat level**: Cinderwatch Pass (generated by `tools/make-cinderwatch-pass.mjs`), a
  combat demo with a camp, a glade near the road, two branches (the Bramble Ruins and the Hollow
  Mire), a quarry and a boss. Packs are placed as groups with a level; a waystone rest brings
  defeated packs back.

## 2. The GLAIVE mechanics that matter

Checked in the rulebook (`GLAIVE_v2.3_Lulu`, pages 4, 8, 22–25, 37–38) and in our code. Only these
rules are needed for the first campaign; nothing below is new rules.

| Mechanic | GLAIVE rule | Where in our code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hitting | Roll d20 + attack bonus; hit when the total is **equal to or higher than** the target's AC. | `Dice.d20`, `Encounter._strike` |
| Criticals | A critical success rolls damage with ADV. A critical failure always misses (and the attacker is off balance). Our code also treats a natural 20 as an automatic hit. | `Dice.d20` |
| Armour | Armour **raises AC**: unarmoured +0, light +2 (25 sp), medium +4 (75 sp), heavy +6 (200 sp), shield +2 (5 sp). Base AC = 10 + DEX. **Armour never reduces damage.** | `rules.js` `ARMOR`, `SHIELD_AC`, `baseAc` |
| Armour piercing | APx: the attacker ignores x points of the target's AC. | not used yet |
| Damage | On a hit, roll the weapon's die. Monster damage is as its stat block says. | `Encounter._strike` |
| Monster stats | `AC, HD (hp = 5 per HD), Atk: number / weapon / attack bonus (damage)`, ML, XP. "If an attack bonus is not given, assume that it is the same as the monster's HD." | `bestiary.js` (`atk` defaults to HD) |
| Unique monsters | GLAIVE tells the GM to make unique individuals (give them talents, vary hp). | `monsterStats` (level, elite) |
| ADV / DISADV | Roll twice, keep the better / worse. | our goblins' `gang` move gives ADV when an ally stands next to the target (a monster talent we wrote, allowed by "devise monster-specific talents") |
| Hit points, injury, death | PC HP = 10 + STR. At 0 or less: out of action, roll the injury table (d12, + ½ the HP below 0). Dead below −(5 + STR). | `rules.js` `injuryResult`, `Encounter._damage` |
| Shields break | A shield can be sacrificed to absorb all damage from one hit. | not used yet |

**What this means for the research design:**

1. "Does the armour stop the attack?" is the same question as "does the attack **hit**?". An
   attack *penetrates* an armour when `d20 + bonus ≥ 10 + DEX + armour AC` (with the natural 1 /
   natural 20 rules). There is no "damage after armour".
2. Damage exists **only on a hit**. A miss is not zero damage; damage on a miss is simply not
   defined. So the game studies two separate variables: *did it penetrate* (yes / no) and *damage
   when it penetrates* (a number).
3. The attack roll does not depend on the target's armour. So if the **strike total** (`d20 +
   bonus`) of an attack is observed, we know whether it would have penetrated *every* armour
   option, not only the one the target wore. This makes one observed attack useful for every
   armour on the shopping list.
4. A monster's attack bonus is part of its stat block. GLAIVE allows it to differ from HD. Giving
   different individuals different attack bonuses and weapons is a way of **writing stat blocks**,
   not a rule change.
5. With a natural 1 always missing and a natural 20 always hitting, every penetration chance lies
   between 5 % and 95 %. No armour on the list makes a citizen safe.

## 4. Conflicts with the current design

Each conflict comes with the smallest revision that solves it.

| # | Conflict | Smallest revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **The UI leaks hidden values.** The target picker shows a foe's `HP 5/5` (its max HP reveals its HD), and the combat log prints the d20 and the bonus separately (`[14]+3=17`), which reveals each enemy's hidden attack bonus. | A *research mode* flag on the combat: foes show a health bar but no numbers; the log shows the strike total only (`strikes 17 vs AC 12`) plus "critical" / "fumble" when they happen. Designer hooks (`window.__game.combat.state()`) keep the truth. |
| C2 | **Packs scale by group level** (`monsterStats` adds HD and AC every few levels). Two goblin packs of different levels are different *stat blocks* for a reason the player cannot see, which breaks "stable, understandable populations". | In research mode the studied family takes its stat block from the population model, not from the group level. Other families keep the old scaling. |
| C3 | **Cinderwatch Pass is a mixed-species combat demo** (slimes, archers, bats, boars, a boss) with few goblins (about 3 per habitat per expedition). | First version: a research overlay on Cinderwatch Pass that uses only goblin packs and adds goblin packs to the road and the mire (generator data). Later: a dedicated small region level (D2). |
| C4 | **The `gang` talent gives ADV** when another goblin stands next to the target. ADV attacks have a different strike distribution. | Record the roll mode (it is observable: the player sees the flanking). The protocol uses only normal-mode attacks (§8). |
| C5 | **Companions kill goblins before they attack**, and goblins flee on a failed morale check. Those enemies never give an eligible attack. | Record them with a missing-data reason (`killed before attacking`, `fled`). Keep HP independent of attack bonus (§7) so this missingness does not bias the estimate. Show the missing count in the ledger. |
| C6 | **XP and silver reward killing**, not careful sampling. | Keep XP (it drives GLAIVE levels). Missions reward the report, not the kill count. |
| C7 | **The boss and the party-of-four balance** are about winning fights, not research. | The research overlay does not use the boss. The party is allowed (it changes missingness, not the population). |

## 6. The smallest playable version

**One complete investigation** with one enemy family, two habitats, two armour options plus
"no armour", and one budget decision.

The loop, mapped to systems:

| Step | What the player does | System |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Royal Steward at the Waystone Camp asks a question and gives a budget | story talk script + mission data |
| 2 | Chooses a route (the road, later the mire) and which packs to fight | the level, a research route marker on the map |
| 3 | Fights | GLAIVE combat (unchanged rules) |
| 4 | — (automatic) | the recorder writes rows from beats |
| 5 | Opens the field ledger: rows, counts, a histogram, summaries | ledger page in the journal |
| 6 | Reports: an estimate (and later an interval) and, separately, a purchase | report dialog with number entry |
| 7 | The kingdom buys armour; a season of raids is simulated | kingdom module |
| 8 | Reads the debrief: research, decision, outcome | debrief page |
| 9 | Next investigation (a harder question) | mission data |

**Missions of the first campaign** (one investigation, five steps):

1. **Define the threat.** The Steward: *"Goblins raid the farmsteads of the Pass. How often does
   a goblin's attack get through light armour?"* The player picks, from short embedded choices,
   the population (goblins of the whole Pass, not "the goblins I meet"), the unit (a goblin), the
   variable (does one attack penetrate AC 12: yes / no; and damage when it does), and which
   observations answer it.
2. **Discover the sampling problem.** At first only the road is open (the mire boardwalk is
   flooded: a story flag). The player gathers a road sample and reports its proportion. Then a
   scout reports raids from the mire edge and the boardwalk opens. A mire sample differs. Task:
   explain why the two samples differ, and whether more road fights would fix the road-only
   estimate (they would not).
3. **Describe the threat distribution.** The ledger shows a histogram of damage on penetrating
   attacks and a bar chart of strike totals. Tasks: compute the mean of the first ten damage
   values by hand (scratchwork, feedback), compare mean and median, read the 90th percentile
   against a citizen's 10 HP, and say why the average alone hides the risk.
4. **Quantify uncertainty.** For light armour, estimate the proportion of eligible attacks that
   penetrate, with an interval (computed by the game now: the hand calculation was mission 3's).
   Then decide: recommend now, or gather more — and *where* (more road data narrows the interval
   but does not fix coverage). A sample-size planner shows n = z²·p′(1 − p′)/E².
5. **Advise the kingdom.** The report: (1) the estimate, (2) the interval, (3) the purchase, (4) a
   one-line rationale picked or written from the evidence. The kingdom acts, a season is
   simulated, and the debrief follows.

## 7. Population model

**World truth** (never shown to the player outside a debrief):

- Target region: *the Pass*, two habitats — **Road** (the glade and the camp road) and **Mire**
  (the Hollow Mire).
- A finite population per habitat for the investigation: *placeholder* Road 120 goblins, Mire 180
  goblins. The share of each habitat in the target population is fixed during an investigation.
- Every goblin is a GLAIVE stat block: AC 13, HD 1 (5 hp), ML 7, one weapon with an attack bonus.
  Only the attack bonus and the weapon vary between individuals. HP does **not** vary (D3).
- Each habitat has a small, readable distribution (*placeholder* values):

| Habitat | Attack bonus | Weapon (damage) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Road | +0: 30 % · +1: 50 % · +2: 20 % | knife 1d6: 60 % · spear 1d8: 40 % |
| Mire | +3: 30 % · +4: 40 % · +5: 30 % | spear 1d8: 60 % · greataxe 1d12: 40 % |

The damage dice are GLAIVE's weapon table (dagger / hand axe 1d6; spear, sword, axe 1d8;
greataxe and other two-handed great weapons 1d12). The data lives in
[`public/research/cinderwatch-pass.json`](../../public/research/cinderwatch-pass.json).

- The weapon is **observable** (the sprite and the log name it). The attack bonus is **hidden**.
- An individual is drawn from its habitat's distribution with a seed made of the world seed, the
  expedition number, the group id and the slot. The same seeds give the same goblins.
- Respawns after a rest are new individuals (a new draw), so each expedition samples again.
- Nothing changes over time in the first campaign.

**Why these numbers work (for review, not for the player).** Against light armour (citizen AC 12)
an individual with bonus b penetrates with probability (9 + b)/20 (clipped to 5–95 %). Road mean
≈ 0.495, Mire mean ≈ 0.65, the whole Pass (40 % road, 60 % mire) ≈ 0.59. A road-only sample of
400 eligible attacks gives an interval of about 0.45–0.54: narrow, confident, and wrong for the
Pass. That is the convenience-sample lesson with numbers the player can see.

**What changes attributes vs what changes observation** (kept apart in code):

| Changes the enemies (world) | Changes what is observed (sampling) |
| --- | --- |
| habitat distributions, population sizes | which habitat the player visits |
| the weapon mix | which packs the player fights or avoids |
| (later) seasons, new breeds | goblins killed before attacking, goblins that flee |
| | the roll mode (flanked attacks are excluded by the protocol) |

## 8. Observation protocol

Every row is a real observation. Hidden values are never written into the player's dataset.

**Tables** (all stored; not all shown at first):

- `enemies`: `enemyId` (unique per individual, survives a pack's uid reuse), `expeditionId`,
  `encounterId`, `family`, `weapon` (observed), `habitat` (where it was met), `gameTime`,
  `fate` (`killed`, `fled`, `escaped`, `still alive when the party lost`), `eligibleAttackId` or
  `missingReason` (`killed before attacking`, `fled before attacking`, `only flanked attacks`,
  `fight lost`).
- `attacks`: `attackId`, `enemyId`, `encounterId`, `targetId` and the target's AC at the time,
  `rollMode` (normal / ADV / DISADV — observable), `strikeTotal`, `critical`, `fumble`,
  `hit` (against its actual target), `damage` (only when it hit; empty otherwise, never 0),
  `eligible` (true for at most one attack per enemy).
- `encounters`: `encounterId`, `expeditionId`, `habitat`, `start / end time`, `result`
  (victory, flight, defeat, avoided), party equipment.
- `expeditions`: `expeditionId`, start and end (a waystone rest), route.

**Definitions** (the ledger's help text says the same in plain words):

- *Penetrates armour X*: the attack is not a fumble and (it is a critical or `strikeTotal ≥
  10 + X`). For citizens with DEX +0: light armour AC 12, medium AC 14.
- *Damage*: the HP the target lost from that hit, measured from the damage roll (not from the
  HP bar, so overkill and healing do not distort it). Defined only for hits.
- *Starting HP*: not recorded. It is not observable in GLAIVE play (and is fixed in this
  campaign anyway).

**The eligible attack** (Mission 4's protocol): for each distinct enemy, its **first attack made
in normal roll mode**. Attacks with ADV or DISADV, free attacks and attacks against an unconscious
target are not eligible.

**What the protocol estimates:** the proportion, over the goblins of the sampled habitats, of a
goblin's normal-mode attack that penetrates armour X — the population mean of each goblin's own
penetration chance. If the goblins met are a random sample of the target population, the eligible
outcomes are independent yes / no results with that common chance, so a binomial model and the
proportion interval are justified. The game shows these assumptions next to the interval.

**Unit-of-analysis guard:** the analysis tools ask for the unit first (enemy, attack, encounter,
expedition). The penetration estimate refuses "all attacks" in Mission 4 and explains why: three
attacks from one strong goblin are not three goblins.

## 9. Statistical targets

All formulas live in one pure module and match what the ledger displays.

| Concept (OpenStax chapter) | Mission | What the player does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population, sample, parameter, statistic, units, variables (1) | 1 | names them for the Steward's question |
| Categorical vs quantitative (1) | 1, 3 | penetration is categorical, damage is quantitative |
| Convenience sampling, undercoverage, sampling variability (1) | 2 | compares road and mire samples; sees that more road data does not help |
| Frequency tables, bar graphs, histograms (2) | 3 | reads the ledger charts |
| Mean, median, percentiles, spread, standard deviation, skew (2) | 3 | computes a mean by hand, then reads the rest |
| Conditional probability, contingency tables (3) | 2–3 (light) | habitat × penetrated table |
| Expected value (4) | 5 | the debrief compares expected losses of policies |
| Sampling distributions, standard error (7) | 4 | a "repeat the expedition" replay in the debrief shows estimates vary |
| Confidence interval for a proportion, margin of error, sample size (8) | 4 | the interval and the planner |

**Safeguards built into the text and the checks:**

- More road data narrows the interval but cannot move it toward the mire (Mission 2 and 4 say so,
  with the player's own numbers).
- The interval is about the **proportion**, not about individual attacks (the help text and an
  interpretation task).
- The average damage alone is not enough when a single hit can drop a citizen (Mission 3).
- Repeated attacks by one goblin are not new goblins (the unit guard).
- A good decision can still meet a bad season (the debrief, §11).
- Each mission lists its assumptions (random sampling within the visited habitats, independence
  across goblins, stable population). Normality is never assumed for damage.

## 10. Kingdom decision and consequence rules

**The decision** (kept separate from the estimate):

- Budget: *placeholder* 2 000 sp from the treasury.
- Citizens at risk: *placeholder* 40 (16 on road farms, 24 on the mire edge). Each has HP 10,
  DEX +0, AC 10 + armour (D7).
- Options (built): one armour type — light (25 sp) or medium (75 sp), GLAIVE's prices — for a
  chosen number of citizens in steps of 5 (and the most the budget allows), or "no purchase".
  The armour goes to the areas in proportion to their citizens (rounded down, the remainder to the
  largest fractions). Unspent silver stays in the treasury.

**The season** (documented, seeded rules; all GLAIVE):

1. Each citizen faces *placeholder* 2 raid attacks, until one drops the citizen. Each attack comes
   from an individual drawn from the **true** population of the citizen's own habitat.
2. Each attack: `d20 + bonus` against the citizen's AC (natural 1 / 20 rules). On a hit, roll the
   weapon damage (a critical rolls it with ADV).
3. A citizen at 0 HP or less is out of action and rolls the injury table (d12 + ½ the HP below 0);
   below −5 the citizen dies. Citizens heal fully between seasons.
4. Losses: *placeholder* an injury costs 100 sp of lost work, a death 2 000 sp and counts as a death.
   Remaining treasury below *placeholder* 500 sp means other needs go unfunded: the shortfall is a
   cost. Cost to the kingdom = silver spent + losses + shortfall.

These placeholders were tuned (2026-10-22) so the decision depends on the evidence: under the
truth, light armour for 35–40 citizens costs the kingdom about 5 000 sp a season against about
5 550 sp for buying nothing and about 6 200 sp for medium armour for 26 (which eats the reserve);
a road-only view of the world makes "buy nothing" look best. With the first values tried (3
attacks, 30 / 400 sp) armour barely mattered and "buy nothing" always won.

The rules are shown in the mission brief, so the player can reason about consequences.

## 11. Debrief

Three separate parts. No single score mixes them.

1. **Research quality** — did the sample cover the target population (habitat shares in the
   sample vs the target)? Was the protocol followed (eligible attacks only, one per goblin)? Is the
   reported interval correct for the data (recomputed)? Was the uncertainty stated? Which groups
   were missing?
2. **Decision quality** — given the player's *own* evidence, was the purchase defensible? Built
   as: every option is replayed over many seasons in "the world as the evidence describes it"
   (each raid attack resamples one of the dataset's eligible attacks and one observed damage
   value, pooled over the habitats the player visited), and in 20 plausible worlds the evidence
   allows (bootstrap resamples of the player's data). A purchase within *placeholder* 5 % of the
   best option in at least *placeholder* 20 % of those worlds is defensible — reasonable for some
   value the evidence allows. (A single evidence world proved too noisy with ~35 goblins: it
   called light armour for all "not defensible" because one resample favoured light armour for 5.) The truth plays no part, so a convenience sample
   can lead to a defensible decision that the truth then punishes — the research part flags the
   sample, the decision part does not.
3. **Realized outcome** — the season that happened: citizens protected, injured, killed, silver
   spent and left, equipment that failed (penetrating attacks on armoured citizens).

**Replay** (advanced): the same policy over many seeded seasons (*placeholder* 300) shows its
typical result and spread, and where the actual season fell. The debrief then unlocks the truth
for this investigation (habitat penetration rates) and compares the oracle choice. Text rules: a
death never "proves" the analysis wrong; a lucky season never "proves" it right.

## 12. Engine boundaries

New folder **`src/game/research/`**, pure and Node-safe like `src/game/glaive/` (no three.js, no
DOM), plus a thin game-side layer:

| Module | Responsibility | Sees the truth? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `populations.js` | habitat distributions from data; draws individual stat blocks from seeds | **yes** (world) |
| `recorder.js` | turns `Encounter` beats into `attacks` / `enemies` / `encounters` rows; applies the eligibility rule | no: only beats the player saw |
| `dataset.js` | the tables, ids, (de)serialization into `CampaignState` | no |
| `stats.js` | counts, frequency tables, histogram bins, mean, median, percentiles, SD (n − 1), proportion, SE, intervals, sample size | no |
| `missions.js` + data | questions, learning objectives, unlocks, required checks | no (refers to truth only by id for the debrief) |
| `report.js` | the report: statistical claim, uncertainty, policy, rationale; validation | no |
| `kingdom.js` | budget, policies, the seasonal consequence simulation (GLAIVE `Dice`) | yes (draws raiders from the population) |
| `debrief.js` | the three-part assessment and the replay | yes, after the report is locked |
| game side: `ResearchMode` in `GlaiveCombat` | research-mode display (C1), stat blocks from `populations` at spawn (C2), expedition boundaries at rests | — |
| UI: ledger page, report dialog, number widget | shows dataset summaries only | no |

**Truth stays sealed:** player-facing modules take a `Dataset`, never a `Population`. A Node test
serializes every player-facing view (ledger data, journal, report form) and fails if any hidden
field (attack bonus, habitat share, population size) appears. Designer views read truth through
`window.__game.research.truth()` (debug only).

### 13.1 Step 1 results

`npm run research:test` (`tools/test-research.mjs`) simulates observations with GLAIVE's own dice
and checks:

- **Truth:** road 0.495, mire 0.65, the whole Pass 0.588 against light armour (AC 12), 0.488
  against medium (AC 14); the exact values agree with a 40 000-attack simulation through `Dice.d20`.
- **Reproducibility:** the same seed parts give the same goblin; the same seed gives the same
  dataset byte for byte; a new expedition draws new goblins.
- **Units:** 40 attacks from 10 goblins count as n = 10 by enemy and n = 40 by attack (flagged
  `clustered`); flanked (ADV) attacks are kept but never eligible; misses have no damage (null,
  never 0).
- **No leak:** the dataset refuses any field that is not an observation (an attack bonus, a
  modifier, the natural die), also when it is loaded from a save.
- **Statistics:** hand-worked values for the mean, median, OpenStax percentiles and quartiles,
  sample and population SD, frequency tables, histograms, invNorm / invT, the proportion and mean
  intervals and the sample-size formula.
- **Sampling (with seeds):** 400 road-only goblins give 0.512 (0.464–0.561), which misses the
  Pass value 0.588. Over 1 000 replications, 95 % intervals from random samples of the whole Pass
  (n = 100) cover the truth 93.5 % of the time; road-only samples cover it 51 % of the time at
  n = 100 and 4 % at n = 400 — more convenient data, worse coverage.

Implementation notes: individuals are drawn as from a large population (with replacement); the
population sizes only set each habitat's share. `Dataset` assigns the eligible flag itself (the
caller cannot set it). `stats.js` also offers the "plus four" and Wilson intervals (Wilson for
designer checks only, D6).

**Open item (before release):** `public/research/*.json` is shipped as a plain file, so a player
who opens the browser's network tools can read the world truth. Fine for development; before
release, bundle the truth into the combat chunk or derive it from a seed at run time, so the
ledger stays the only player-facing source.

## 14. Investigation 2: where should the armour go? (planned)

Status: **planned 2026-10-22, not built.** Loop step 9: the consequences of investigation 1 create
the next, harder question. Chosen with the owner: option A ("where should the armour go?") with a
small piece of option B ("did the armour work?") in its debrief.

### 14.1 The question and why it follows

After investigation 1's season the Steward sees that the mire farms lost more people than the road
farms. Next question: *"Should both areas get the same armour? How often do attacks get through
medium armour in each place, and how should the 2 000 silver be split between the road and the
mire?"*

- **Population:** the goblins of each area separately (two populations), not the Pass as a whole.
- **Unit:** one goblin, its eligible attack (the same protocol, §8).
- **Variables:** penetrates light (AC 12) and medium (AC 14): yes / no; the area (categorical).
- **Targets:** each area's proportion against medium armour, and the **difference** between the
  areas (mire − road), with an interval.
- **Policy:** per area — an armour type and a count for the road farms (16 citizens) and for the
  mire edge (24) — within one budget.

### 14.2 Curriculum (OpenStax)

- Two independent proportions and the interval for their difference (ch. 10, the two-population
  bridge; built on ch. 8's single-proportion interval).
- Conditional probability and contingency tables (ch. 3), from the last season: injured or not, by
  armour and by area.
- Why a per-area decision needs data from each area; why an interval for the difference that
  includes 0 means the data cannot yet tell the areas apart.
- First look at confounding (sets up Phase 4): in last season's table armoured citizens may look
  no safer, because the armour went to the area with the stronger goblins.

### 14.3 What the player does (missions)

1. **Two populations** — choices: what the new report is about (each area's goblins, separately),
   and why the Pass-wide estimate from investigation 1 cannot answer it.
2. **Reuse the old data?** — the ledger already holds each goblin's strike total, so it already
   says whether each attack would have beaten medium armour (AC 14). Choice: is reusing it fair?
   (Yes for these goblins and this protocol — the strike total does not depend on the armour worn;
   but only as fair as the places it came from.) Then compute one area's medium-armour share by hand.
3. **Compare the areas** — after at least 15 eligible goblins per area: the two shares and their
   difference with a 95 % interval (computed). Choice: what an interval for the difference that
   does / does not include 0 tells.
4. **Last season's table** (the piece of option B) — the 2 × 2 table of armoured / unarmoured ×
   injured / unhurt from investigation 1's season, overall and per area. Tasks: one conditional
   probability by hand (P(injured | armoured)); choice: why "armoured citizens were hurt just as
   often" does not prove armour useless (the areas differ: confounding).
5. **Advise the kingdom** — the report: the two estimates and the difference (with intervals),
   and a per-area purchase.

### 14.5 Numbers to check while building (placeholders)
